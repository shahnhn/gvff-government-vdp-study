# An Empirical Study of Government Vulnerability Disclosure Programs

## Multi-Dimensional Evaluation of U.S. Federal Agencies

This repository contains the research materials, evaluation framework, and supporting dataset for the study.

The study examines the maturity and operational characteristics of Vulnerability Disclosure Programs (VDPs) implemented by selected U.S. federal agencies. It applies the **Government Vulnerability Disclosure Program Feature Framework (GVFF)** to transform VDP policy characteristics into measurable variables and support structured, descriptive comparison across agencies.


## Research Objective

Government Vulnerability Disclosure Programs provide formal channels through which security researchers can report vulnerabilities to public-sector organizations. However, the structure, accessibility, legal protections, scope, and operational maturity of these programs may vary across agencies.

This study aims to:

* Evaluate the maturity and operational characteristics of selected U.S. federal agency VDPs.
* Apply a structured, multi-dimensional framework to assess measurable VDP features.
* Identify patterns and variations in VDP implementation across the study sample.
* Examine areas in which agency VDP policies demonstrate stronger or weaker alignment with established disclosure practices.
* Provide a reproducible dataset and evaluation framework for future research on government vulnerability disclosure.


## Research Framework

The study uses the **Government Vulnerability Disclosure Program Feature Framework (GVFF)**, a structured evaluation framework designed to convert VDP policy characteristics into quantitative variables.

The framework consists of:

* 8 evaluation dimensions
* 52 measurable features*
* Feature-level coding criteria
* Dimension-level evaluation
* Agency-level maturity scoring

The framework supports systematic comparison by translating publicly available VDP policy information into a standardized dataset.

The evaluated dimensions address multiple aspects of VDP design and operation, including policy accessibility, reporting mechanisms, scope, legal protections, communication practices, remediation-related commitments, and program governance.


## Methodology

This research follows a **quantitative descriptive and comparative approach**.

The study process included:

1. Identifying eligible U.S. federal agencies.
2. Collecting publicly available VDP policies and related official documentation.
3. Mapping agency policies against the GVFF feature criteria.
4. Coding the presence, absence, or applicable status of each measurable feature.
5. Calculating agency-level and dimension-level maturity measures.
6. Comparing observed patterns across the selected agencies.
7. Interpreting findings within the scope and limitations of the dataset.

The analysis is primarily descriptive. The study does **not** use inferential statistical testing to establish statistical significance. Therefore, findings are presented as observed patterns, differences, variations, and comparative trends within the study sample.

## Important Limitations

The findings should be interpreted within the following limitations:

* The study uses a limited sample of 12 agencies and does not represent all U.S. federal agencies.
* The analysis is based primarily on publicly available policy documentation.
* Published policies may not fully reflect an agency’s internal vulnerability-handling practices.
* The presence of a policy feature does not necessarily demonstrate its operational effectiveness.
* Some policy language may require researcher interpretation during feature coding.
* VDP policies may change after the dataset was collected.
* The study uses descriptive analysis and does not make claims of statistical significance.

These limitations are acknowledged to support transparent interpretation and appropriate use of the findings.
